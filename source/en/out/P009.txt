<p><strong>וְדִינָא יִתִּב</strong> (but the judgment will be set): the same sentence pattern as 7:10, “the judgment was set.” In this chapter the sentence repeats and forms a “frame”: 7:10 and 7:26 both say “judgment sits,” and judgment is placed between two “sittings.”</p><p><strong>כָּל־שָׁלְטָנַיָּא לֵהּ יִפְלְחוּן וְיִשְׁתַּמְּעוּן</strong> (all dominions will serve and obey him): who “him” is, the original may refer to “the Most High,” or to “the people of the saints” or to the “son of man.” This ambiguity is deliberate: the kingdom is given to the saints, yet it is also God’s kingdom. See the issues.</p><p><strong>בְּלִבִּי נִטְרֵת</strong> (I kept the matter in my heart): “kept” (נטר) means “to guard,” “to preserve.” This verb recalls Luke 2:19, where Mary “kept all these sayings, pondering them in her heart.” Daniel did not at that moment tell the vision to everyone; he first kept it in his heart.</p>
@@2637@@
<p><strong>This chapter ends not with a shout but with one person keeping it in his heart.</strong></p><p>Verse 26 says: “But the judgment will be set.” This echoes 7:10: judgment is “seated.” Before, there was the rushing, warring, devouring of the beasts, a scene of chaos; now there is one who sits. “They will take away his dominion, to consume and to destroy it to the end.” This is the final announcement against the little horn. Notice that it is not a war but a verdict: not “defeated” but “judged.”</p><p>Then the most important sentence of the chapter: “The kingdom and the dominion, and the greatness of the kingdoms under the whole sky, will be given to the people of the saints of the Most High.” This sentence echoes 7:14, where the son of man receives the kingdom: on one side “him,” on the other “the saints.” <strong>The kingdom is not taken by force but given; not given to the strongest but to the saints of the Most High.</strong> All the greatness will be given to them. It is the gentlest and also the most subversive sentence: all the kingdoms of the earth will in the end be handed to those who never scrambled for them.</p><p>“All dominions will serve and obey him.” The “serve” and “obey” in this sentence stand opposite chapter 3, where everyone served the golden image. Chapter 3 was forced service; in chapter 7 all powers freely serve the true kingdom. This is the conclusion of the whole long Aramaic section of the book: God’s kingdom is everlasting, and all who hold power will in the end serve him.</p><p>Finally, Daniel. “Here is the end of the matter,” he says, and he himself was “much troubled, and his face was changed.” Even a man who had seen so great a vision was frightened. Yet he said: “but I kept the matter in my heart.” He did not hurry to explain, and did not hurry to proclaim. He first kept it. This is something we can learn: some words God gives us not to be spoken at once but to be kept in the heart first, so that they change us. <strong>When we cannot yet understand, we can first keep it, and go on being faithful.</strong></p>

I. The Four Winds of Heaven and the Beasts from the Sea (7:1–8)
@@3005@@
Brothers and sisters, peace to you. Have you ever been jolted awake in the middle of the night by a dream? Your heart racing, your palms sweating, you sit on the edge of the bed and tell yourself: it was only a dream. But the picture is still there. Today we read a dream that frightened even Daniel. The six chapters before were his story in other people’s courts; from chapter 7 on, it is the vision he himself saw, in bed.
@@3006@@
The text says: “In the first year of Belshazzar king of Babylon Daniel had a dream and visions of his head on his bed. Then he wrote the dream and told the sum of the matters.” Notice this act: he wrote the dream down. Not to frighten people, but to leave it for those who would come after. So this chapter was written for us. Then the vision begins: “I saw in my vision by night, and, behold, the four winds of the sky broke out on the great sea.” In the Bible the sea is often a symbol of unrest and surging, the place where the nations churn. But look: whose wind is it? Heaven’s wind. Even the chaos of the sea has a hand above it.
@@3007@@
Then four great animals came up from the sea. Notice the direction: not coming down from heaven but growing out of chaos. The first was like a lion with eagle’s wings, the king of beasts joined to the king of birds, how majestic. But the text goes on: “I watched until its wings were plucked, and it was lifted up from the earth, and made to stand on two feet as a man. A man’s heart was given to it.” Wings plucked, made to stand, given a man’s heart. Is this picture familiar? In chapter 4, an exalted king was brought low and then given a man’s heart. And look: these verbs are all passive: plucked, lifted up, given. Even a beast is treated this way; how much more a man. This means we need not put all our eyes on the beast’s teeth.
@@3008@@
The second was like a bear, with three ribs in its mouth between its teeth, and “they said this to it: ‘Arise! Devour much flesh!’” Even the command to devour was spoken by someone. The third was like a leopard, with four wings and four heads, “and dominion was given to it.” Given: it too was given. Its speed was not its own ability. Do you hear it? Not one of the four beasts seized its own power. This is the first reminder this chapter gives us: even the fiercest power is permitted.
@@3009@@
The fourth does not even have a name, because no animal can be compared to it: “awesome and powerful, and exceedingly strong. It had great iron teeth. It devoured and broke in pieces, and stamped the residue with its feet,” and it had ten horns. Then another horn came up, a little one, “in this horn were eyes like the eyes of a man, and a mouth speaking great things.” About these beasts and this horn there have long been various views; we set them side by side in the issues, without charting a timetable and without identifying anyone today. What the text wants us to see is the feature: it speaks big words. Have you ever met in life a voice so loud that it seems no one can answer it? This dream wants to tell you: the mouth that speaks big words will not have the last word. Such voices often appear not only in the dark; they may appear in an office, in a family, even in a church meeting. So today we are not here to match verses to people and look for who the little horn is; we first ask: in my own words, is there a bit of big talk meant to prop myself up?
@@3010@@
All four beasts are “given”; how does this help me face pressure?
@@3011@@
It helps this way: you need not see pressure as a power with no limit and no master. The text uses passive verbs to tell us: wings plucked, a man’s heart given, dominion given, and even the command to devour has a source. This does not say the pressure does not hurt, and does not say oppression is good; it says it is not the last word. When you face a very great power, you can first ask: who is permitting it? Does it have a limit? Asking this slowly turns trembling into quiet prayer. You can also write in a journal one power that frightens you now, and beside it one sentence: this is permitted, and it has a limit. After writing this a few times, you will find that a distance slowly appears inside you.
@@3012@@
II. The Throne Is Set Up, and the Books Are Opened (7:9–12)
@@3013@@
The rhythm of the text changes at verse 9. Before, it was one beast after another; now it is one sentence: “I watched until thrones were placed, and one who was ancient of days sat.” No clamor, no army, only a throne, quietly set up. The beasts are busy scrambling for position; in heaven a place was long since prepared.
@@3014@@
The one sitting on it is the Ancient of Days, literally “the aged of days.” This is not the old of decline but the old of one who was there before all days. The beasts before were all newcomers; he is the one who has always been. “His clothing was white as snow, and the hair of his head like pure wool. His throne was fiery flames, and its wheels burning fire.” This is purity and also holiness, so holy that no one dares look at it. Then the numbers: “Thousands of thousands ministered to him. Ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him.” These numbers are not for you to count but for you to feel: in heaven there is an order we cannot see, larger than all the armies of earth.
@@3015@@
Then the most comforting words of this passage: “The judgment was set. The books were opened.” Judgment is carried out seated, unalarmed, unhurried. The books are opened, and nothing is missed. Have you ever been wronged? Have you ever seen something that no one dealt with? Have you ever thought: is this just going to be left like this? This verse is speaking to you. It is not meant to frighten you but so that you can let go, and hand the matter of revenge to the One who sits.
@@3016@@
Verses 11 and 12 speak of the end: “the animal was slain” because of “the voice of the great words which the horn spoke.” You see, the little horn’s greatest power was its mouth, and its end came because of its mouth. As for the rest of the beasts, “their dominion was taken away; yet their lives were prolonged for a season and a time.” Here is a gentle detail: the other beasts had their dominion taken away, yet their lives were prolonged. God’s judgment has measure. Even the season and the time were not set by them. This passage changes history’s end from a free-for-all into a throne that was set up. So when the world feels chaotic, you can first place one picture in your heart: a throne has been set up; someone has already sat down. Your prayer is not speaking into thin air; it is spoken to the One who sits.
@@3017@@
Will the picture of “judgment” make me more afraid, not more at rest?
@@3018@@
It depends on which side you read from. The picture of the text is: the Judge sits, the books are opened, the mouth of evil is stopped, and the wronged are vindicated. For the oppressed this is rest; for someone with a debt on his heart it is an invitation to come back into the light. We need not turn this scene into a threat, and we need not use it to find excuses for our own revenge. What you can do is bring your wrongs and your debts before the One who sits, and say: please deal with this, and have mercy on me. If you have a very concrete debt in your heart, do not put it off; today in quiet, tell God about it sentence by sentence; and if you need to apologize to someone, take one small step this week.
@@3019@@
III. Light from the Original: One Like a Man, the Saints, Given (7:13–14, 18, 27)
@@3020@@
The original of this chapter has three groups of words worth reading slowly. The first is “one like a son of man,” Aramaic כְּבַר אֱנָשׁ (kevar enash). “Son of man” is literally “son of a man,” and in Aramaic it very often means simply “a human being”; with “like” in front it is “one like a human being.” Set it against what came before: the beasts were “like a lion,” “like a bear,” “like a leopard,” and this one is “like a man.” Human kingdoms in history have often been beastly; the kingdom God establishes has a human face.
@@3021@@
The second is “brought near.” Aramaic הַקְרְבוּהִי (haqrevuhi) means “was brought near.” He did not walk up to the throne by himself; he was brought before the Ancient of Days. Then “dominion was given him, and glory, and a kingdom”; that verb is the same kind of passive as the beast’s “dominion was given to it.” The beast devours; the son of man receives. The beast seizes; the son of man is entrusted. The true kingdom is not taken by force; it is entrusted.
@@3022@@
The third is “serve” and “receive.” Aramaic פְּלַח (pelach, to serve): in chapter 3 everyone was commanded to serve the golden image; in this chapter “all the peoples, nations, and languages should serve him.” The same words, two kingdoms: one forced, one truly worthy. And “receive,” Aramaic קַבֵּל (qabbel): in 7:18, “the saints of the Most High will receive the kingdom,” is the same verb as Darius “received the kingdom” in chapter 5. The kingdom is something received.
@@3023@@
These three groups of words together are the route of this chapter: like a man, brought near, given. You can ask yourself: do I often try to get a position by seizing, by big words, by my own strength? God’s way of giving to us is receiving. This is an honest question: it is not that we do not need a place, but that we need to discern which places God gave and which I grabbed for myself.
@@3024@@
How does the title “Son of Man” connect to Jesus in the New Testament?
@@3025@@
The New Testament makes the connection. At his trial Jesus said to the high priest: “you will see the Son of Man sitting at the right hand of Power, and coming on the clouds of the sky.” Many see here a link to Daniel 7:13. We follow the New Testament in saying so, and we also respect the different readings that have long existed, as an individual, as a representative of the saints, or as both, which are set side by side in the issues. We do not set the son of man against the saints: Jesus is King, and he also makes those who belong to him receive the kingdom. Reading it this way lets us see him more, and also see ourselves more as those who are given. So when someone asks you, “Who is the son of man?”, you need not hurry to give one only answer; you can say: I see this one brought to God and given the kingdom; and I too am among those who are given.
@@3026@@
IV. One Like a Son of Man, and the Saints Receive the Kingdom (7:13–28)
@@3027@@
We come to the high point of the whole book. “I saw in the night visions, and behold, there came with the clouds of the sky one like a son of man, and he came even to the ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. Dominion was given him, and glory, and a kingdom, that all the peoples, nations, and languages should serve him. His dominion is an everlasting dominion, which will not pass away, and his kingdom that which will not be destroyed.” Before, beasts came up from the sea; now one like a man comes with the clouds of heaven. The kingdoms of the beasts rise and fall; this kingdom is everlasting and will not pass away. This joins in one line with the words of chapters 2, 4 and 6. These words give everyone in a troubled age a steady direction.
@@3028@@
Then what about Daniel? The text says: “my spirit was grieved within my body, and the visions of my head troubled me.” A man who did not waver in the lions’ den this time was frightened by a dream. Faith is not the absence of feeling. But he did one thing: “I came near to one of those who stood by, and asked him the truth concerning all this.” He did not pretend to understand and did not guess by himself; he came near and asked. The thing you fear most can also be brought before God in this way. This is what real faith looks like.
@@3029@@
The attendant’s explanation was short: “These great animals, which are four, are four kings, who will arise out of the earth. But the saints of the Most High will receive the kingdom, and possess the kingdom forever, even forever and ever.” The word “But” is the turn of the whole explanation: before, beasts that rise; after, an everlasting kingdom. The saints’ receiving the kingdom is also a receiving. The people you belong to may be very small in the world’s eyes, but the text says they will receive the kingdom. This is not a hope with pride in it but a hope with humility: we receive the kingdom not because we are strong but because the kingdom is given.
@@3030@@
What Daniel most wanted to know was the fourth beast, because that one was the hardest to face. He was honest; you do not need to pretend you are not afraid. The attendant said this king “will speak words against the Most High, and will wear out the saints of the Most High. He will plan to change the times and the law; and they will be given into his hand until a time and times and half a time.” All three actions put God somewhere else: speaking big words belittles God; wearing out the saints attacks those who belong to God; changing the times and the law means rewriting the norms God has set. “A time and times and half a time” is in the original “a time, two times, and half a time”; we do not decide its length for it, and we do not count days; what we see is the direction: the saints given into his hand is limited, bounded, and has an end.
@@3031@@
Notice this turn: “until the ancient of days came, and judgment was given to the saints of the Most High, and the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom.” What you have suffered was not suffered in vain; what you are waiting for is not something you will wait for in vain. The text does not say which day, so we do not count. And finally: “But the judgment will be set, and they will take away his dominion, to consume and to destroy it to the end. The kingdom and the dominion, and the greatness of the kingdoms under the whole sky, will be given to the people of the saints of the Most High.” All the kingdoms of the earth will in the end be handed to those who never scrambled for them. It is the gentlest and also the most subversive sentence.
@@3032@@
Then Daniel: “my thoughts much troubled me, and my face was changed in me; but I kept the matter in my heart.” He was still alarmed; he did not hurry to explain and did not hurry to proclaim; he first kept it. Some words, when God gives them to us, are not to be spoken at once but to be kept in the heart first, so that they change us. When we cannot yet fully understand, we can first keep it, and go on being faithful. This is something we can practice every day: take one thing received today, do not rush to say it, keep it in the heart first, and in prayer let God slowly make it clear to us.
@@3033@@
If the saints are “overcome,” is the hope of receiving the kingdom empty?
@@3034@@
No. 7:21 says plainly that this horn made war with the saints and prevailed against them; the text is honest and does not say believers are never hurt. But then comes “until”: until the Ancient of Days came and the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom. The hope is not that suffering is removed right now, but that suffering has a limit, and that One who sits in judgment is watching. For those who are suffering this is comfort; for those who are not it is a reminder: do not make light of, or make use of, someone else’s suffering. Remember also that this hope is a hope held with patience: you need not deny pain, and need not force yourself to look strong; you only need, in the pain, to keep an open heart toward the One who sits in judgment.
@@3035@@
The first deviation is to match the four beasts one by one to certain nations or organizations of today, and set out a timetable. The four beasts have long been read in different ways, and these notes set them side by side without charting a schedule. The point of this chapter is “given and judged,” not to make people count days.
@@3036@@
The second deviation is to identify the little horn with a person or regime of today and call him the antichrist. The text describes features: speaking big words, wearing out the saints, planning to change the times and the law, and names no one of today. Identifying someone makes the congregation panic and also hurts the innocent. The fruit of this chapter is patience, not panic.
@@3037@@
The third deviation is to teach “one like a son of man” as having only one acceptable answer, belittling other readings, or setting the son of man against the saints. The son of man and the saints have long been read as individual, as collective, and as both; in the New Testament Jesus calls himself the Son of Man, and we see the connection while respecting each view.
@@3038@@
The fourth deviation is to teach “the saints will receive the kingdom” as “believers will surely win and not be hurt.” 7:21 says plainly that this horn made war with the saints and prevailed against them; receiving the kingdom comes after the judgment and is given, not an exemption from suffering right now.
@@3039@@
The fifth deviation is to use the theme of judgment to frighten people, or to find excuses for our own revenge. The Judge sits to give judgment, and the books are opened; our responsibility is to hand the matter to him, not to pick up the sword ourselves.
@@3040@@
How should I respond when someone is keen to use this chapter to chart a “timetable of the last days”?
@@3041@@
You can first affirm his zeal for God’s word, and then gently return to the purpose of the text. This chapter says plainly that the description of the four beasts and the little horn is so that the saints would know: power is given and will be judged, and the kingdom will be given to the saints of the Most High. The text gives no date and does not ask anyone to count. You can ask him: does this way of arranging things make you love the Lord more and be more patient, or more anxious and more eager to persuade others? If the latter, it is time to return to the text. Finally, one reminder: discussion can have warmth, but should not have winners and losers. If you can accompany the other person back to the text, and back to loving the Lord and loving others, that conversation is already a very good witness.
@@3042@@
The first question: we are very used to “those who read the times are wise.” Facing a very great power, we have learned to watch which way the wind blows, not to stick our necks out, and to protect ourselves first; in a Chinese setting this is often a wisdom, and also the experience of long years of protecting family. This chapter does not ask us to be reckless, and does not ask us to confront; it asks us to see that the wind is heaven’s wind and the throne is set up. You can be cautious, you can be quiet, but you need not let “the way things are going” become your god.
@@3043@@
The second question: we often say “bear it for a moment and the waves will calm,” and we are used to swallowing a wrong. Some people carry the pain for years because of it; some finally explode in revenge. This chapter gives a third way: the books are opened, and the Judge sits. Handing the wrong to him is neither suppression nor revenge but speaking it honestly, and then letting go, into his hand. If the matter involves harm or violence, please also find safe people and professional help, and do not carry it alone.
@@3044@@
I am used to “bearing it”; how is that different from “handing the wrong to God”?
@@3045@@
“Bearing it” is often swallowing the words and going on hurting; “handing it to God” is saying the words out loud, to the One who will listen. The difference is whether there is someone to say them to. Each day you can use three sentences to tell God: how this hurt me, how I want to deal with it, and now I hand it to you. When needed, find a reliable person or a counselor to walk with you. Handing it over does not mean the matter is not important; it means you no longer use your own strength to carry a judgment that was never yours to carry. Also, if you are in a setting where you cannot speak, you need not force yourself to hand it over by speaking; writing it down, praying, or talking with a safe person are all possible ways.
@@3046@@
VII. Living Out 321: The Throne Has Been Set Up
@@3047@@
The three foundations: Jesus is my Role Model. Jesus called himself the Son of Man and said, “For the Son of Man also came not to be served, but to serve”; he gains the kingdom not by devouring but in the form of a servant. The Bible is my Standard, because our attitude toward the four beasts, the little horn and the son of man does not rest on guessing but only on what the text itself says: given, judged, the kingdom given to the saints. The Holy Spirit is my Guide, because before great powers we do not need more panic; we need a Spirit who is with us, so that I can be quiet and still be faithful.
@@3048@@
The two core values: Let Jesus be King means that before all big words and pressure we still keep our focus on the throne, acknowledging that the kingdom, dominion and glory are all given. Let Jesus receive all the glory means that when we receive the kingdom, we do not say it is our merit but say: he gave it. The one purpose: to build God’s system. The saints of the Most High do not scramble but receive; in the chaos they remind one another: the throne has been set up, and the kingdom will surely be given.
@@3049@@
Changing our mindset changes our life: when we think “power decides everything,” our life is chased by the teeth of the beasts; when we know “the throne has been set up,” our life has rest. From self to selflessness: the person with self props himself up with big words and power; the selfless person sets down power, returns to his place, and lets man be man and God be God. This is not a slogan but a daily practice of returning to one’s place: today, have I handed over what is not mine?
@@3050@@
How do “selflessness” and “seeing the throne” connect?
@@3051@@
They connect this way: someone who sees the throne no longer needs to be a throne himself. People with self are often afraid: afraid of being crushed, afraid of being overlooked, so they prop themselves up with big words and control. To see the throne is to see that someone is seated and someone is in charge; I can return to my place, do what I should, and hand over what is not mine. Then selflessness is not losing myself but no longer spending strength to hold a position that does not belong to me. A very simple practice: every night before sleep, ask yourself two questions: today, was there something I tried to prop up? What can I hand over? Then, in bed, say it in one sentence.
@@3052@@
VIII. Four Kinds of Hearts, One Throne
@@3053@@
The proud type says, “I will use big words and bearing to let people know I am not to be trifled with.” This heart may also be afraid of being looked down on. The text reminds us: the mouth that speaks great things is finally stopped by judgment. You can ask yourself: are my words propping myself up, or serving others?
@@3054@@
The fearful type says, “The power before me is too great; I dare not speak, and I dare not believe.” This fear is real; even Daniel was alarmed. Please, like him, come near and ask; bring the thing you fear most before the throne. If your situation is truly dangerous, first protect yourself and others, and still stay open to God in your heart.
@@3055@@
The ashamed type says, “What I have done in the past, if the books were opened, how could I face it?” Please remember that the One who opens the books is also the One who gives the kingdom to one like a son of man. You can honestly bring your debts before him, asking for mercy and for cleansing, instead of hiding.
@@3056@@
The wounded type says, “I have been deeply hurt, no one has vindicated me, and I dare not trust again.” That wound is real. The text does not ask you to pretend nothing happened; it says: one sat, and the books were opened. Please first find a safe person for yourself and tell your story; do not ask yourself to forgive at once, and do not force yourself to have faith at once. God does not hurry you.
@@3057@@
First, every day spend five minutes bringing one power that frightens you before God, saying only: the wind is heaven’s wind, and the throne has been set up.
@@3058@@
Second, write down the most recent sentence that made you shrink or made you want to speak big words, and write down one small step back to your own place.
@@3059@@
Third, find a companion and agree to remind each other each day with one phrase: the throne is set up; no comparing, no showing off.
@@3060@@
Fourth, take one revenge, accusation or worry you cannot give away, hand it in one prayer to the One who sits in judgment, and do not bring it up again.
@@3061@@
Fifth, in the setting where you are, do one small, faithful thing that does not grab for power or speak big words, so that your witness builds up in ordinary days.
@@3062@@
I declare: the wind is heaven’s wind; the throne has been set up; I need not be chased by the teeth of any beast.
@@3063@@
I declare: I will not prop myself up with big words; I return to my place, letting man be man and God be God.
@@3064@@
I declare: the books are opened, and I can hand my wrongs and my debts to the One who sits in judgment.
@@3065@@
I declare: the kingdom is given, not seized; I will receive it and be faithful, until Jesus reigns as King.
@@3066@@
How can I make “the books are opened” a daily practice, and not a distant picture?
@@3067@@
You can use five minutes in the evening. For the first minute, be still and say: Lord, you are seated. Then say out loud the heaviest thing of the day, whether what someone did to you or what you did to someone. Then in one sentence hand it over: this matter, I give to you. Finally, if there is an apology or reconciliation needed, write down one small step for tomorrow. Done this way, the books are no longer carried by you alone but placed each day before the One who sits. You can do it slowly, not all at once; even if you only get halfway, God sees, and encourages you to come again tomorrow. That is enough.
@@3068@@
Daniel chapter 7 begins with a dream in the night: four beasts in the sea, and in the end a throne quietly set up; one like a son of man is brought before him and given the kingdom; the saints of the Most High will receive the kingdom forever. The teeth of the beasts are real; the throne is real too. What this chapter gives us is not more guessing about the beasts but more trust in the throne.

Have you ever, on some night, asked heaven: how long? Daniel chapter 8 has a ram, a male goat, and a little horn, one after another exalting themselves, the last destroying the sanctuary and taking away the continual burnt offering. Even the holy one in heaven asks: how long will this be? The answer is a number made of evenings and mornings. Then Gabriel comes, calls Daniel “son of man,” touches him as he lies in a deep sleep of fright, and sets him upright. This chapter asks us: when you cannot understand and no one can tell you the days, do you hurry to calculate, or do you let God raise you up?

The nights are numbered; what God gives is understanding, not a schedule of days. When we are frightened and fallen asleep he reaches out and sets us upright, and we can return to our post.

In the third year of King Belshazzar, Daniel in a vision stood by the river Ulai at Susa and saw a ram with two horns pushing west, north and south; no beast could stand before it, and it did as it pleased and magnified itself. A male goat came from the west, not touching the ground, with a notable horn between his eyes, and broke the ram’s two horns; when he was strong, the great horn was broken, and four came up in its place. Out of one of them came a little horn, growing toward the south, the east and the glorious land, reaching to the host of heaven, taking away the continual burnt offering and casting down the sanctuary, and prospering in all it did. A holy one in heaven asked: how long? The answer: two thousand and three hundred evenings and mornings, then the sanctuary will be cleansed. Gabriel was commanded to make Daniel understand; he was frightened and fell on his face, was called “son of man,” and was touched and set upright. The explanation: the ram is Media and Persia, the male goat is Greece; later a king of fierce countenance arises, and is broken without hand. The vision is sealed up. Daniel was sick for some days, then rose up and did the king’s business.

When We Do Not Understand, Let God Raise Us Up; After Being Raised, Return to Our Post
@@3119@@
Toward God: this week, be quiet for five minutes each day, tell God one “how long?”, and then say only one sentence: “Please set me upright.”
@@3120@@
Toward yourself: write down one thing for which you are anxious to know the day or the outcome, and write down one small thing you can do today, so that you are not trapped by this question.
@@3121@@
Toward others: find a companion and agree to remind each other each day with one phrase: “the nights are numbered”; do not pass around schedules of days, and do not frighten one another.
@@3122@@
Toward daily life: on days when you do not understand, are very tired or are sick, still do the one duty in front of you, such as going to work on time or caring for your family.
@@3123@@
Toward mission: when someone wants to use this chapter to chart a timetable, gently bring him back to the purpose Gabriel gives: to make people understand, not to make them panic.
@@3124@@
① Wrong teaching: working out the two thousand three hundred as calendar dates, or matching it to some event of today
@@3125@@
Right understanding: the original is “two thousand and three hundred evenings and mornings”; it has long been read as 2,300 days, as 1,150 days, and as a symbolic number; these notes set them side by side and do not count days.
@@3126@@
Test: is the congregation trusting God more and better able to endure, or more eager to guess at dates?
@@3127@@
② Wrong teaching: identifying the little horn or little king with a person or regime of today, and calling him the antichrist
@@3128@@
Right understanding: what the text gives is features and an end: speaking great things, casting down the sanctuary, attacking the saints, broken without hand; it has long been read as a king of the Greek period, as a figure of the last days, and as a pattern; these notes set them side by side and identify no one of today.
@@3129@@
Test: is the congregation more watchful and patient, or more fearful and more ready to put labels on people?
@@3130@@
③ Wrong teaching: confusing the “son of man” of 8:17 with the “one like a son of man” of chapter 7
@@3131@@
Right understanding: 8:17 is a Hebrew form of address meaning that Daniel is a finite human being; chapter 7 is Aramaic and describes the one who comes with the clouds of heaven. The wording and the position of the two differ, and we do not force them together.
