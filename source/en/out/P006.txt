This gospel means we need not run from the balances, because we do not stand there by our own weight.</p>

I. A Lively Night, a Hand With No Body (5:1–12)
@@2057@@
Brothers and sisters, peace to you. Have you ever had an experience like this: something you already knew in your heart how to do, yet you kept saying “wait a little”? Perhaps a call you should have made, an apology you should have spoken, a habit you should have laid down. Each time you had a reason, each time you felt that today was not the best moment. In this lesson we read of a man whose “wait a little” finally waited until the last night.
@@2058@@
Daniel chapter 5 begins at a great feast: “Belshazzar the king made a great feast to a thousand of his lords, and drank wine before the thousand.” Imagine the scene: the lamps blazing, laughter without end, wine cup after cup. The text does not say this night was unusual in any way; judgment often draws quietly near on just such a night. Then comes that decision: “Belshazzar, while he tasted the wine, commanded that the golden and silver vessels which Nebuchadnezzar his father had taken out of the temple which was in Jerusalem be brought.” Notice the moment: while he tasted the wine, not when he was sober, but when the wine was at its height.
@@2059@@
These vessels were from the temple of God in Jerusalem, originally for serving God. Now they were brought for amusement, to raise a cup to gods made of gold, silver, bronze, iron, wood and stone. The most dangerous thing is often not one great sin but, in a relaxed moment, treating holy things as things for amusement. Perhaps it is not gold and silver vessels; it is a stretch of time originally meant for serving the Lord, a gift originally meant for him, a Lord’s Day that originally belonged to him, and in relaxed moments we casually use it elsewhere.
@@2060@@
Then verse 5 says: “In the same hour, the fingers of a man’s hand came out and wrote near the lamp stand on the plaster of the wall of the king’s palace.” Not an angel, not a voice, only a hand, part of a hand. It wrote opposite the lamp stand, in the place where the light was brightest. God does not need to say much; one hand is enough. The king’s face was changed, his thoughts troubled him, the joints of his thighs were loosened, and his knees struck one against another. The thousand lords, the gold and silver vessels, the feast: none of it mattered any more. When God’s hand appears on the wall, the strongest part of a person is often the first to give way.
@@2061@@
He did what his predecessors had done: he summoned the wise men and promised a great reward: purple, a chain of gold, the third place. But the wise men could not read the writing. Not only could they not interpret, they could not even read. Human wisdom, before God’s handwriting, could not take even the first step. Just when the whole room was in panic, one person came in: the queen mother. She was the only one in the whole scene who spoke quietly: “O king, live forever; don’t let your thoughts trouble you, nor let your face be changed.” She did not scream and did not blame; she only remembered, and she remembered a name: Daniel. Some people, in the midst of chaos, are not the strongest but the ones who remember.
@@2062@@
Please bring this picture back into your own life: is there a place where, when you are most relaxed, you most easily loosen your grip? Perhaps a weekend night, a dinner, a moment when no one sees. Between the holy and the ordinary there is often no clear wall; it is a very small voice in our heart, at that moment, that reminds us: this is the Lord’s. May we be willing to hear that voice.
@@2063@@
What do the vessels of the temple mean for us today?
@@2064@@
The New Testament says that the believer’s body is the temple of the Holy Spirit, and our time, gifts and money are also entrusted to us by God. This chapter does not ask us to hold a special superstition about any object, but reminds us that holy things must not be treated as casual things. You can ask yourself: which part originally prepared for God have I grown used to using casually? Find it, and hand it back to God in prayer; this is the lesson of this chapter for us. You can begin with the thing you use most often, such as your phone or your calendar, and ask: is there a part of this that is God’s, which I have taken for amusement?
@@2065@@
II. Not Speaking for a Reward, Speaking Only for the Truth (5:13–24)
@@2066@@
Daniel was brought before the king. The king asked him: “Are you that Daniel of the children of the captivity of Judah?” Listen to this address: “of the captivity.” This king remembered only his label, not his record. People are often like this; they remember only your label, and not what you have done. Daniel did not argue, and did not state his credentials. The king repeated that great reward: purple, a chain of gold, the third place. This was an invitation of power: if you speak, you will be the third man in this kingdom.
@@2067@@
Listen to Daniel’s answer: “Let your gifts be to yourself, and give your rewards to another. Nevertheless, I will read the writing to the king, and make known to him the interpretation.” He did not rage and did not bargain; he only said very clearly: this is not why I came. Only one who does not speak for a reward can say the truth others do not like to hear. Because what he was about to say was not good news.
@@2068@@
He spoke first of history: God gave Nebuchadnezzar the kingdom, greatness, glory and majesty, and all peoples, nations and languages trembled before him; but his heart was lifted up, and he was deposed from his kingly throne, lived with the wild donkeys and ate grass as oxen, until he knew that the Most High God rules in the kingdom of men. This is the whole story of chapter 4, condensed into a few sentences, spoken to this night and this man. Then he turned and pointed directly at Belshazzar: “You … have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this.”
@@2069@@
Please hear this sentence slowly. He did not say “you did not know”; he said “you knew, and did not bow.” The greatest debt is not ignorance, but knowing and still not humbling oneself. This sentence is especially important for us today: we have heard much truth, read many verses, know many stories; what we lack is often not information but a bowed head. Is there one thing you know very clearly God has already spoken about, yet you are still putting it off, still finding reasons?
@@2070@@
Then he made the charge concrete: you lifted yourself up against the Lord of heaven, you brought the vessels of his house and drank from them, you praised gods that cannot see or hear, and you have not glorified the God in whose hand your breath is. This sentence is very near: every breath you take is in God’s hand. At last he said: “Then the part of the hand was sent from before him, and this writing was inscribed.” The writing on the wall was not without cause; it had a reason, a charge and an interpretation. God’s judgment is never sudden; it comes after long patience.
@@2071@@
When Daniel said all this, the king’s thousand lords were all present. He did not avoid the crowd, and did not soften it with a roundabout manner. But there is a delicate point in his words: he began with what the king knew well, with the king’s father, with the history the king had seen with his own eyes. A truly wise reminder is not to throw down a new accusation, but to set before a person again what he already knew.
@@2072@@
Daniel refused the rewards, so why did he later put on the purple? Is that not a contradiction?
@@2073@@
It is not. What he refused in 5:17 was “speaking in order to get a reward”; the purple of 5:29 was ordered by the king himself after he had finished. The order is important: he did not change his interpretation because of a reward, and did not ask the king for anything beforehand. He put on the purple and was still only a servant; that very night the king was slain, and everything changed hands again. What we can learn is not to treat a reward as a condition of faithfulness; and when a reward comes, not to treat it as our own credit. Remember too that his interpretation was very clear yet had no mockery in it; this is the pattern we who speak the truth should learn.
@@2074@@
III. The Light of the Original: Know, Balances, Wanting (5:22, 25–28)
@@2075@@
The original of this chapter has three words worth reading slowly. The first is know, the Aramaic יְדַע (yeda). It recurs in chapter 4: “that the living may know that the Most High rules in the kingdom of men.” Nebuchadnezzar paid the price of seven times to “know”; Belshazzar “knew all this,” yet paid no price and did not humble himself. The same word, in these two chapters, speaks of two kinds of people: one learned in the low place, one would not learn in the high place.
@@2076@@
The second is balances, the Aramaic מֹאזַנְיָא (mozanya). TEKEL: you are weighed in the balances. The picture of balances in the Old Testament is often connected with fairness: “just balances.” The balances are not meant to shame people, but to show true weight. You may resist this picture, or you may accept it: God’s standard is real, and also fair.
@@2077@@
The third is wanting, the Aramaic חַסִּיר (chasir), meaning lacking, deficient. “You are weighed in the balances, and are found wanting.” Notice that this word does not say “you are bad” but “you are not enough.” No one before God is enough. This “not enough” is the starting point of the gospel of the whole Bible: only those who are not enough need a Lord who makes up for us.
@@2078@@
These three words together are the route of this chapter: to know, then to be weighed, then to be shown wanting. But we need not stop here. The New Testament says that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us, and in this God’s love toward us is shown. The balances are still real, but there is one who already stands on our side.
@@2079@@
Today you can quietly place these three words in your own situation: what do I “know”? By what have I been “weighed”? Where am I “wanting”? Then lay these three answers one by one before the cross. You will find that what truly sets a person free is not having no shortfall, but knowing that someone has already paid it in full.
@@2080@@
Are “MENE, TEKEL, UPHARSIN” units of weight, or verbs?
@@2081@@
Both readings are possible. The four words of the original can be read as three units of weight, mina, shekel and half-shekel, or as three verbs, numbered, weighed and divided. Daniel’s interpretation takes the second, and brings out a wordplay: “divided” and “Persia” share the same root. We need not dwell here too long; the point is: what people see is writing, what God means is a verdict, and it takes the Spirit of God to point it out. In this way, our reading of the original is not for showing off knowledge but for hearing God more clearly. If you want to study further, you can ask a pastor or consult different commentaries, and set the views side by side.
@@2082@@
Do the balances mean we must reach God’s standard by our own deeds?
@@2083@@
No. The purpose of the balances is to show a person’s shortfall so that he knows he needs grace. The gospel of the New Testament does not cancel God’s standard, but says that Christ has already met that standard for us: he died for us, and rose again. We do not stand by our own weight, but by his. So facing the balances, we can honestly say: I am not enough, and can also say with peace: he is enough. This answer lets us on the one hand take the standard seriously, and on the other rest in grace; neither can be missing. So facing the standard, we need not run from it nor boast; we need only honestly rely on Christ.
@@2084@@
IV. The Balances and That Night (5:25–31)
@@2085@@
Now let’s look at the writing on the wall. “MENE, MENE, TEKEL, UPHARSIN.” Did the wise men fail to read it because it was too hard? Or because it was too direct? These words could be a merchant’s ledger, or God’s judgment. Daniel saw the second at a glance. He spoke it sentence by sentence: MENE: God has counted your kingdom, and brought it to an end. Notice, it is not “your kingdom is about to end,” but “God has already counted.” This is a process that began long ago: God, in patience, had been counting all along.
@@2086@@
Then comes the theme of the chapter: “TEKEL: you are weighed in the balances, and are found wanting.” You are weighed in the balances, and what is on the other side is not another person but God’s standard. Every person will be placed on these balances. This sentence is very solemn. But notice that when Daniel spoke of the shortfall, there was no mockery and no gloating. He only said it very clearly. This is how one who interprets judgment should be: not pleased, not exaggerating, only faithfully proclaiming.
@@2087@@
The last word: “PERES: your kingdom is divided, and given to the Medes and Persians.” This is the result. Then the text goes on: Belshazzar commanded that Daniel be clothed with purple, a chain of gold put about his neck, and a proclamation made that he should be the third ruler. This king kept his promise; he did not kill the one who brought the word, yet he did not repent either. His action was a perfectly complete courtesy, without one repentance.
@@2088@@
Then comes the shortest narrative of the whole book: “In that night Belshazzar the Chaldean King was slain.” Just this one sentence, no description, no scene. This night began with a great feast and ended in a sudden change of dynasty. God’s judgment is not always loud; it can be quiet, yet on time. The last verse: “Darius the Mede received the kingdom, being about sixty-two years old.” The name changed, but have you noticed: the Most High rules in the kingdom of men, and gives it to whomever he will. This kingdom is still this kingdom.
@@2089@@
Daniel, that night, put on the purple, and before daybreak the king had changed. He did not rise higher because of it, and lost nothing because of it, for he knew that his position had never been given by the king; it was given by God. This is the best rest for us whenever our position rises or falls. Your name card will change, your title will change, and your identity will be redefined by others; but the God in whose hand your breath is has never changed.
